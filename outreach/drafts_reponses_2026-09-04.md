> Subject: Re: Still looking, or already sorted?

Hi,

Thank you, this is the kind of feedback I actually wanted. Both points are fair and both are going into the next version of the checklist.

On the My Number card, you are right that the line assumes you already know what it is. It is the individual number issued to every resident, including foreign residents, once you register your address at the ward or city office. The number itself arrives by post; the physical card is optional but worth getting, because it is the piece that makes tax filing, opening or updating a bank account, and most online government procedures straightforward instead of tedious. Banks are now required to hold it on file. I will spell that out on the line rather than assume it.

On Wise versus OFX, the honest answer is that the checklist reflects the most common case rather than yours, and I should say so on the line.

Wise gives you the mid-market rate and charges a visible fee on top, so what you pay is easy to check. It also gives you JPY account details, which is convenient for receiving money in Japan. For a pension or a monthly living allowance, moderate amounts arriving regularly, it is usually the cheapest and by some distance the simplest.

OFX works differently. There is typically no upfront transfer fee, and the cost sits inside the exchange rate they quote you. That is less transparent, but on large one-off sums the margin brokers quote tends to tighten with size, and you get a named dealer plus tools like forward contracts to lock a rate ahead of a purchase. Buying and remodelling a house is exactly the scenario where that can beat a percentage-based fee, and the ability to fix a rate months in advance can matter more than the spread itself when you are committing to a purchase price in yen.

So I would not tell you one is superior in the abstract. I would compare them on the day, on the amount you are actually sending, and on one number only: how many yen land in the Japanese account. Not the fee, not the advertised rate, the final figure. Both will quote that before you commit. For a house purchase it is worth getting quotes from both, and from your UK bank as a baseline, on the same afternoon.

One thing worth knowing well ahead of your timeline: large incoming transfers to a Japanese account attract source-of-funds questions from the receiving bank, and a purchase-sized sum from abroad will usually need documentation ready. It is routine, but it is smoother when you have anticipated it rather than discovered it.

Hokkaido in retirement with a house to remodel sounds like a good plan. A year out is not too early to start watching the market up there, since the akiya and older-stock side of it moves on very different logic from the city rental market I track.

Good luck with it, and thanks again for taking the time to write.
