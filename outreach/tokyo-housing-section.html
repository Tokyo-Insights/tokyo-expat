Where Families Actually Live: Housing Around Tokyo's International School Clusters

Once you have shortlisted schools, the housing decision is largely made for you. Tokyo's international schools sit in a handful of areas, and each cluster comes with a different rent level and a different daily commute. Here is what the market currently looks like around them.

Central Tokyo (Minato: Hiroo, Azabu, Moto-Azabu). This is where the shortest school runs are, and where the rent is highest. The median advertised rent for a two-bedroom flat (2LDK in Japanese listings) is around 410,000 yen a month, and a three-bedroom (3LDK) around 600,000. In exchange you get buildings used to foreign tenants, English-speaking services nearby, and children who can often reach school without a long bus ride. Families on a housing allowance concentrate here.

The western wards (Setagaya, Meguro, Suginami). This is where most families end up. Setagaya runs around 232,000 yen for a 2LDK and 285,000 for a 3LDK, Meguro around 300,000 and 375,000, Suginami around 205,000 and 258,000. You get noticeably more floor space, quieter streets and far better park density, at the cost of a 20 to 40 minute commute toward the central schools.

Yokohama. If your school is in Yokohama, the economics change completely: around 135,000 yen for a 2LDK and 160,000 for a 3LDK, roughly half of Setagaya. If your school is in Tokyo, treat Yokohama with caution. The savings are real, but so is the daily journey.

The commute trade-off families underestimate. International schools have no catchment areas, so the constraint is not the train line, it is the school bus route. Routes cover specific zones, and an address five minutes outside one can mean a parent driving twice a day for years. Confirm that your shortlisted address is on the route before you sign anything. And test the journey at 07:30, not at 10:00 on a viewing day: the same trip is a different experience with children and a rush-hour train.

A practical rule: pick the school first, then let the map narrow your options, then compare rents within that narrowed set. Doing it in the other order is how families end up moving twice.

Rent figures are medians from active rental listings, compiled by Tokyo Expat, which publishes a ward-by-ward and station-by-station rent index for Tokyo.
