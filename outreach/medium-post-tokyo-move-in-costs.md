# What It Really Costs to Move Into a Tokyo Apartment (2026 Budget Breakdown)

The rent is the number everyone checks first, and it is the number that matters least in your first month. What actually drains a newcomer's budget in Tokyo is the pile of upfront costs that never appears on the listing. I operate rentals in Tokyo, and the single most common surprise I watch people absorb is the size of that first payment. Here is what to actually budget, so it does not catch you out.

## The standard lease: four to five months of rent, upfront

On a normal unfurnished apartment, you do not pay one month to move in. You pay a stack:

- **Deposit (shikikin):** usually one to two months. Partly refundable at move-out, minus legitimate cleaning or damage.
- **Key money (reikin):** often one month, and this one is a gift to the landlord. You do not get it back.
- **Agency fee:** typically one month plus tax.
- **First month of rent**, often prorated, plus the coming month.
- **Guarantor company fee:** around half a month if you use one, which most foreigners do.
- **Fire insurance and a lock change:** a smaller fixed sum, but real.

Add it up and a standard move-in commonly lands near four to five months of rent, paid in one transfer. On a 120,000 JPY apartment, that is roughly 500,000 to 600,000 JPY before you have bought a single piece of furniture.

## The furnished route: one month or less

This is precisely why furnished share houses and monthly units exist. They compress that entire stack into something close to a single month, sometimes with just a small deposit and no key money, no agency fee, and no guarantor. You pay more per square metre, but the cash you need on day one drops dramatically. For most people arriving without a large local buffer, that trade is worth it for the first few months.

## The costs people forget entirely

- **Furniture and appliances.** An unfurnished Japanese apartment can mean no fridge, no washing machine, no light fixtures, and no curtains. Budget a few hundred thousand yen if you are furnishing from zero, or sidestep it with a furnished unit.
- **Utility setup and internet.** Small individually, but they cluster in the first weeks.
- **Move-out restoration.** Plan for a cleaning fee at the end. Normal wear is meant to be the landlord's cost, so keep dated photos and question anything vague on the final bill.

## A simple rule for your first Tokyo budget

Before you sign anything, know your cash-on-hand for month one, then match the housing type to it. If you can comfortably cover four to five months of rent plus furniture, the standard market is open to you. If you cannot, start furnished, keep your cash, and move to a standard lease once you have built a local income record. Either path works. Choosing the one your budget can actually absorb is what keeps the move from starting in the red.

Tokyo is not an expensive city to rent in month after month. It is an expensive city to move into. Plan for the move, and the rest is manageable.
