Your budget is not really what decides this. What decides it is whether you try to rent a normal apartment, because that is where a working holiday year quietly falls apart.

If you go the usual route, the monthly rent is the small problem. A 1K across Tokyo's 23 wards sits around 100,000 yen, closer to 78,000 or 85,000 out east in Edogawa, Adachi or Katsushika, and 135,000 to 145,000 in Chiyoda, Chuo or Minato. The part that hurts is getting in at all: deposit, key money, agency fee, fire insurance and a guarantor company routinely add up to three to five months of rent before anyone hands you a key.

The bigger wall is the length of your visa. Guarantor companies and plenty of landlords look at how long your residence card runs, and a one year working holiday gets turned down often enough that your bank balance never becomes the question. I would not build the plan around getting your own place.

So the realistic options are a share house or whatever your Worldpackers or Workaway hosts put you in. Share houses take you without a guarantor, without key money and usually without a deposit, and you can leave on a month's notice, which matters a lot when a host falls through. A private room in Tokyo is usually somewhere between 50,000 and 80,000 depending on how far out you are, and utilities and wifi are often included.

One thing I would plan for instead of discovering it: if a host cancels and you need a room at short notice in spring, you are competing with the entire March intake and the cheap rooms are gone by the end of January. Having two or three share houses shortlisted before you fly is worth more than the extra thousand euros you are trying to save.
