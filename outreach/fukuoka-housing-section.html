What Family Housing Actually Costs Around Fukuoka's International School Areas

Once you have a school in mind, Fukuoka narrows the housing search fast. The city is compact enough that almost every ward is commutable, so the real question is not "can we get there" but "what does each area cost, and what are we giving up". Here is what the rental market looks like today, ward by ward, for family-size flats.

Chuo, the centre. The most expensive ward, and the newest housing. A two-bedroom (2LDK in Japanese listings) runs around 167,000 yen a month at a median 60 sqm, a three-bedroom (3LDK) around 190,000 yen at 77 sqm. Listings sit a median seven to eight minutes' walk from a station and the typical building is only eight years old. Families who want to walk to everything concentrate here.

The western wards: Sawara, Nishi, Jonan. The seafront side of the city, and where a lot of family housing sits. Sawara runs about 118,000 yen for a 2LDK and 140,000 for a 3LDK, Nishi about 93,000 and 112,000, Jonan about 90,000 and 114,000. You get more space and quieter streets, in buildings typically 17 to 27 years old.

Hakata, Higashi and Minami. Hakata is around 130,000 yen for a 2LDK, Higashi 92,000 and 133,000, Minami 90,000 and 105,000. One number deserves a warning. Hakata's three-bedroom median, 129,500 yen, reads as cheaper than its two-bedroom, and that is not a bargain: the three-bedroom stock there has a median age of 21 years against 8 for two-bedrooms. You are pricing older buildings, not a discount.

How this compares with Tokyo. A three-bedroom in Fukuoka's dearest ward costs less than a two-bedroom in Setagaya, a mid-market Tokyo ward where the median is around 230,000 yen for roughly the same floor area. Families weighing one city against the other can check that directly in our ward-by-ward rent index for Tokyo.

The trade-off families underestimate. Three-bedroom supply is thin and older everywhere except Chuo. Across the city there are roughly two two-bedroom listings for every three-bedroom one, so a family that needs three bedrooms in a newer building is really choosing between Chuo prices and an older building elsewhere. Decide which of the two you are willing to bend on before you start viewing, because the market will not offer you both in most wards.

A practical order of operations: fix the school, let it narrow the wards, then compare rents inside that shortlist. Doing it the other way round is how families end up moving twice.

Rent figures are medians from roughly 12,700 active family-size rental listings across Fukuoka's seven wards, compiled by Tokyo Expat, which publishes rent data for Japanese cities.
